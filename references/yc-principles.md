# YC decision filter — source-derived, not a mandatory process

The user supplied 4 complete Paul Graham essays and a condensed Michael Seibel guide. For full paragraph-by-paragraph original text and notes, see `sources/`, and for extensive chapter extraction see `references/yc-comprehensive.vi.md`.

## Principles for actual product development
1. Solve a real, pressing problem (especially your own or one you know deeply). Ask who needs this **now** and would try even version one.
2. Start with a narrow, intense group of users; expand after delighting them. Don't start with broad lukewarm hypothetical interest.
3. Ship an initial useful version rather than modeling months of speculative features; use real user feedback to correct assumptions.
4. Work directly with initial users: observe behavior, help onboard, do manual work where appropriate, proactively delight.
5. Clear owner, ONE main development-cycle goal, prioritized must-have scope, shared written decisions and compact specifications, instrumentation and tests.
6. Test the actual end-to-end experience before release; keep short feedback loops, revisit actual outcomes, iterate.
7. Engineering craftsmanship means correct behavior and reliable tests, not premature generalized architecture; do not 'play startup' through ceremonies, fundraising theater or overlong roadmap.
8. Founder judgment: use real user/problem expertise and people instincts; be determined and honest about limits/risks.
9. Trade resources carefully: manual work is okay until demand warrants automation; early adoption/learning outranks scaling architecture.
10. **Historical qualifications**: The Paul Graham essays contain dated investment, risk, social and employment claims. Treat them as the author's 2007–2014 opinions, not universal or current factual guidance.

## Not all YC text belongs in a coding workflow
- Founding a company, fundraising, personal/family risk, age, cofounder guidance and macroeconomic claims are preserved in the comprehensive source guide, **not** copied into mandatory execution gates.
- Historical Socialcam 2-week mobile cycles and final 3-day QA are illustrations, not fixed deadlines; a one-day prototype should proportionately reserve QA time rather than copying 3 days.
